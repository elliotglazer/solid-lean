# Solid Idealized Lean

Working paper. Proofs are at sketch level unless marked otherwise; §8 records, claim by claim, what is proved, what is sketched, and what is machine-checked. Nothing here has been refereed.

## Introduction

**The result.** We specify an idealized dependent type theory L_ann — the kernel type theory of Lean, with a fixed stock of inductive types in place of inductive declarations, annotated so that the universe level and the proof/data kind of every subterm can be read off the syntax — and extend it by an effective *representation scheme* to a theory L_solid. The main theorem (Theorem 7.2, via Theorem 2.1) is that L_solid is bi-interpretable with H, the many-sorted theory of a tower of ZFC universes cut at consecutive inaccessibles, and is therefore *solid* in Enayat's sense: whenever a model M of L_solid interprets a model N, N interprets a model P, and M ≅ P by an M-definable isomorphism, then M ≅ N by an M-definable isomorphism. Solid theories are tight — no two distinct extensions of the theory in its own language are bi-interpretable — so L_solid determines its models up to definable isomorphism in the way ZF or PA does and ordinary type theories do not. Concretely, L_solid decides the type identities Lean leaves open (Nat ≠ Int, Bool → Unit ≠ Nat → Unit) and makes transport along an equality the identity on codes (§6.4), and every proposition of L_solid is provably equivalent to a set-theoretic one (§7.4). The tower half of the theorem (H is solid) and the solidity of the relational form T_L of L_solid are machine-checked in Lean 4 for the fragment of the calculus described in §8.

**What is new relative to Lean.** Lean's theory is its kernel type theory with three axioms, `propext`, `Quot.sound` and `Classical.choice`. The kernel theory is essentially the same in Lean 3 and Lean 4: Carneiro's thesis describes and models Lean 3's; Lean4Lean specifies Lean 4's, which adds definitional eta for structures, nested inductive types in the kernel, and literal arithmetic on `Nat` and `String` — the additions being exactly the features L_ann omits, so the comparison below applies to both. L_ann and L_solid differ from Lean's theory in the following ways, and in no others.

1. *Annotation.* Binders and applications carry the kind (prop or data) and the universe levels; judgments Γ ⊢ t : A @ r carry the level r of the type. Lean's terms are unannotated, and the kind and level of a term are recovered by type inference from a derivation; here they are computed from the syntax (the classifier, §3.1), and Lemma 3.2 says the computation agrees with every derivation. This is what makes the unique-typing problem of Lean4Lean irrelevant to the semantics. Certificates are derivation trees, checked not inferred; no decidability is claimed (§3.3).
2. *Universes.* Concrete levels n ∈ ω only: no level variables, no max/imax, no universe-polymorphic terms; a universe-polymorphic declaration is an effective family of ground instances. U_0 = Prop is impredicative, as in Lean. Universes are non-cumulative with explicit lifts, as in Lean, but Lift, up and down are primitive constants with reconstruction rules rather than the inductive type ULift.
3. *Inductive types.* There are no inductive declarations. A fixed stock is primitive — False, Σ, finite sums, Eq, propositional truncation, W-types, Acc, quotients, and indexed families obeying the discipline of §3.4 — each with its eliminator and computation rules. Excluded: nested inductives, structure eta beyond single-constructor records, and Lean's general inductive declarations, which a translator from Lean would have to compile into the stock (§8).
4. *Axioms.* propext and quotients (Quot, mk, lift, sound, ind) are as in Lean, with Type-valued quotients taken modulo the least equivalence closure. Classical.choice — a chosen element of every nonempty type, as data — is replaced by propositional choice (a truncated choice function) together with unique choice, and excluded middle is primitive rather than derived by Diaconescu's argument. Function extensionality holds in the intended semantics, where functions are their graphs.
5. *Definitional equality.* Typed and level-preserving: β, η for data functions, ζ, δ, definitional proof irrelevance (as in Lean), and the registered computation rules of the stock. K-like reduction is recovered from the computation rule of Eq.rec on refl together with proof irrelevance. Not included: definitional eta for structures, the literal reductions on Nat and String, and the kernel's untyped conversion algorithm; conversion is a rule applied with a certificate, not an algorithm (§3.2).
6. *The representation scheme* (L_solid only). Constants code_n : U_{n+1} → Z_n and value_n : (A : U_{n+1}) → A → Z_n into an internal set tower, with six axiom schemes (§6.1) saying that they form a bijection from typed elements onto set membership, respect universes and truth values, carry every certified operation to its set-theoretic description, and act naturally on the internal tower itself. Nothing in Lean corresponds to them, and they have no computational behaviour (§7.6). They are what turns Lean's soundness (a model in set theory) into reconstruction (the model determines the type structure), and reconstruction is what solidity needs.
7. *Strength and intended models.* "ω inaccessibles" must be read as a schema, not a sentence. The sentence "there is an ω-sequence of inaccessible cardinals" is strictly stronger than the schema {"there are at least n inaccessible cardinals" : n ∈ ω}, since it proves that every finite fragment of ZFC plus the schema has a model. Lean's universe levels are external — a proof uses finitely many universes and needs finitely many inaccessibles — so Lean has the strength of ZFC plus the schema (Carneiro), and cannot state the sentence. The same holds of H, whose sorts are externally indexed: no sort contains all the κ_n as a set, every finite fragment of H has a model in ZFC + n inaccessibles, and H is equiconsistent with the schema. Both theories have intended models built from an ω-sequence of inaccessibles κ_0 < κ_1 < ⋯, whose existence is the sentence: Lean's universes interpreted by V(κ_n) for any such sequence, H's sorts by V(κ_n) for one satisfying CINP — consecutive, with no greatest inaccessible below κ_0 (§2.1, axioms 3–4). The strength is the same; the difference is that H pins the semantics to one ω-block of consecutive inaccessibles, which is what solidity requires and what Lean's theory leaves open.

## 1. Framework

### 1.1 Theories

All theories are first-order and many-sorted, with sorts indexed by ω. The index set is external — a set of the metatheory, not an object of any theory — so there is no quantification over sorts, and a statement made "for every sort" is a scheme, one sentence per sort. A theory may use only finitely many of the sorts (ZFC, and each sort of H on its own, are single-sorted). Classical logic with equality on each sort. A theory may have infinitely many symbols and axioms; every theory in this paper is recursively axiomatized, and "T proves φ" always means derivability, never truth in a standard model.

### 1.2 Finitary interpretations

Let Σ and Σ′ be signatures. A finitary interpretation I of Σ′ in Σ assigns to each sort s′ of Σ′ a finite list of Σ-sorts (its arity), a domain formula δ_{s′} and an equivalence formula ε_{s′} on tuples of that arity, and to each relation, function and constant symbol of Σ′ a Σ-formula of the matching arity; all formulas may use a fixed finite list of parameters p̄ and each mentions finitely many Σ-sorts. An admissibility formula α(p̄) specifies the allowed parameters. For a Σ-structure M and admissible p̄, I(M, p̄) is the Σ′-structure with universes δ/ε. I interprets T′ in T if T ⊢ ∀p̄ (α(p̄) → I(φ)) for every axiom φ of T′, where I(φ) is the usual translation. Interpretations compose, and the composite is finitary. When N = I(M, p̄) we write M ⊳ N.

A definable isomorphism between Σ′-structures presented inside M is a family, one map per Σ′-sort, each given by an M-formula with parameters and finite sort support, such that the family preserves and reflects every symbol. "M-definable" always means this; no single formula ranges over the external index set of sorts. For a theory using finitely many sorts this is Enayat's notion exactly, via the usual rendering of sorts as disjoint predicates on one domain — the convention Enayat makes explicit for two-sorted KM in Remark 2.6 of [Enayat-C], where an interpretation may "entangle" the sorts; the passage between a per-sort family of formulas and a single formula is a finite disjunction. For theories using infinitely many sorts — Enayat's theories of types Z_ω and KM_ω (Remark 2.8 of [Enayat]) and the theory H below — the definition is not spelled out in [Enayat], and the two available readings differ: the one-sorted rendering admits quantification over the union of all sorts, which is quantification over the index set in disguise, while the family reading admits non-uniform per-sort families that no single formula generates; neither reading implies the other. The family reading is the one under which the level-by-level argument behind Remark 2.8 goes through, since each level's isomorphism is defined from the previous one by applying comprehension in N to a formula of finite support, and it is the reading adopted here. Every claim of solidity below is a claim about this category and no other.

Definition 1.1 (Enayat). T is solid if for all models M, N, P of T with M ⊳ N ⊳ P and an M-definable isomorphism i : M → P, there is an M-definable isomorphism M → N. (Enayat writes M* for P; the letter is changed here only to keep subscripts readable.) T and T′ are bi-interpretable if there are interpretations F of T′ in T and D of T in T′ such that T proves that a specified definable map ε is an isomorphism D(F(A)) → A, and T′ proves that a specified definable map ρ is an isomorphism M → F(D(M)).

Lemma 1.2 (transfer). If T is solid and T′ is bi-interpretable with T, then T′ is solid.

Sketch. Given M ⊳ N ⊳ P models of T′ with M-definable i : M ≅ P, apply D and use ρ to transport the interpretations: D(M) ⊳ D(N) ⊳ D(P), with a D(M)-definable isomorphism D(M) ≅ D(P) obtained from D(i). Solidity of T gives a D(M)-definable isomorphism D(M) ≅ D(N); apply F and conjugate by ρ on both sides to get an M-definable isomorphism M ≅ N. Every map in the chain is sortwise definable with finite support, so the composite is finitary. This is Enayat's Remark 2.1.1 carried out in the present category; the only thing to check beyond his argument is that finite support is preserved by composition, which is immediate.

### 1.3 The bounded-presentation lemma

The tower theory H of §2 has the property that each sort is a set of the next. The following lemma is the only form in which "work in a sufficiently high sort" is ever used below.

Lemma 1.3. Let φ(x̄) be an H-formula whose sorts all have index ≤ a, and let b > a. There is a formula φ^b in the language of the single sort U_b, with the sets j_{c,b}[U_c] (c ≤ a) as parameters, such that H ⊢ φ(x̄) ↔ φ^b(j(x̄)). Consequently: (i) for any interpreted sort of any finitary interpretation in H, with support ≤ a, its domain modulo its equality is a set of U_b for every b > a, by Separation and Power Set in U_b; (ii) any class of U_c (c ≤ a) defined by such a φ is a set of U_b, and if it is a subclass of an element of U_c it is an element of U_c, because U_c is a rank segment of U_b; (iii) Foundation and induction in U_b apply to every such class.

Sketch. Induction on φ: relativize quantifiers over sort c to the set j[U_c], replace the membership relation of sort c by the membership relation of sort b using that j is a membership isomorphism onto its image, and replace the transition maps by inclusions. The parameters are the images of the lower sorts, which are sets of U_b by H's axiom 2. The consequences use that j[U_c] is the rank segment V(κ_c) of U_b, which is transitive and closed under subsets.

The lemma is what licenses Separation for predicates built from interpretations and isomorphisms: any such predicate has finite support, so it defines a set of some higher sort.

## 2. The tower theory H and its solidity

### 2.1 Axioms

H has sorts U_n for n ∈ ω, a binary relation ∈ on each sort, function symbols j_n : U_n → U_{n+1}, and constants κ_n of sort U_{n+1}. Write V(α) for the rank segment of an ordinal α inside a sort. Axioms:
- (U_n, ∈) satisfies ZFC, for each n (a scheme).
- In U_{n+1}: κ_n is strongly inaccessible and j_n is a membership isomorphism from U_n onto V(κ_n).
- In U_{n+2}: κ_{n+1} is the least inaccessible above j_{n+1}(κ_n).
- In U_1: there is no greatest inaccessible below κ_0.

Axioms 3 and 4 together are the CINP condition (consecutive inaccessibles, no predecessor): the κ_n are consecutive, and κ_0 is admitted if it is the first inaccessible or has limit index, and excluded if it has successor index. In an ambient ZFC universe with infinitely many inaccessibles, U_n := V(κ_n) for the first ω inaccessibles is a model. H is recursively axiomatized.

Three consequences are used freely. (a) Transport: by Lemma 1.3, lower sorts are transitive, subset-closed sets of higher sorts, and every finitary-definable class of a lower sort is a set of a higher one. (b) Absoluteness: for an ordinal α in a lower sort, "α is inaccessible", "β is the least inaccessible above α", and "no greatest inaccessible below α" have the same truth value in that sort and in every higher sort, because all the functions and subsets these statements quantify over already lie in the lower sort (a rank segment of inaccessible height contains every subset and every function between its elements). (c) Named ladder: in U_b, the inaccessibles in the interval [κ_0, κ_{b-1}] are exactly κ_0, …, κ_{b-1}, by axiom 3 and absoluteness.

Theorem 2.1. H is solid in the category of §1.

Proof. Let M ⊳ N ⊳ P be models of H with an M-definable isomorphism i : M → P. Write I for the interpretation of N in M and J for that of P in N. All parameters are fixed once. The proof produces an M-definable isomorphism M → N sort by sort. Every assertion below is a statement provable in the relevant theory from the axioms and the definitions of I, J, i; the phrase "in N_b" means "as a sentence of the sort U_b of N, obtained by Lemma 1.3".

Step 1: internal collapse. Fix n. The sort P_n has a J-domain with finite support in N, say ≤ a; take b > a. By Lemma 1.3 the domain modulo J-equality is a set D_n of N_b, and the interpreted membership is a set relation E_n on D_n. E_n is extensional in N_b because P satisfies extensionality. We claim N_b ⊨ "E_n is well-founded": every nonempty X ⊆ D_n with X ∈ N_b has an E_n-minimal element. Fix such X. Since N is interpreted in M with finite support, X is an element of some M-sort presented by a tuple ξ, and "y ∈ X" (membership of N_b) is an M-formula in ξ. Consider the class

    P := { u ∈ M_n : i_n(u) ∈ X }

of M. It is M-definable with finite support (it uses i, J read through I, and the parameter ξ), so by Lemma 1.3 it is a set of a sufficiently high sort M_d, and it is nonempty because i_n : M_n → P_n is onto and X is nonempty. Foundation in M_d gives u ∈ P with no element of P below it. Then i_n(u) is E_n-minimal in X: if y E_n i_n(u) with y ∈ X, write y = i_n(v); since i is a membership isomorphism, v ∈ u in M, so v is in the class, contradicting minimality. This is the only place i is used, and it is used to prove that a collapse exists, not to define one.

Inside N_b, ZFC proves that a set-sized well-founded extensional relation has a unique Mostowski collapse. So there is an N_b-definable isomorphism e_n : (D_n, E_n) → (S_n, ∈) onto a transitive set S_n of N_b. As a map on the interpreted sort, e_n is N-definable with finite support; through I it is M-definable.

Step 2: transitions. The map j_n of P, read in N, embeds D_n as a transitive subset of D_{n+1} (axiom 2 for P). In a sort of N containing both collapses, e_{n+1} restricted to the image of D_n is a membership isomorphism onto a transitive subset of S_{n+1}, hence another collapse of (D_n, E_n); by uniqueness, e_{n+1} ∘ j_n = e_n and S_n is a transitive subset of S_{n+1}.

Step 3: power-set correctness. Put h_n := e_n ∘ i_n : M_n → S_n, an M-definable membership isomorphism. Claim: if a ∈ S_n and y ⊆ a with y an element of any N-sort, then y ∈ S_n. Write a = h_n(x). The class

    b := { z ∈ x : h_n(z) ∈ y }

is M-definable with finite support, hence by Lemma 1.3(ii) an element of M_n (it is a subclass of x ∈ M_n). Since h_n is a membership isomorphism onto the transitive set S_n, h_n(b) = { h_n(z) : z ∈ b } = { w ∈ a : w ∈ y } = y. So y = h_n(b) ∈ S_n. In particular the internal power set of a in S_n equals the true power set of a taken in any N-sort, and the same argument shows every function and relation between elements of S_n belongs to S_n.

Step 4: the collapsed sorts are rank segments at inaccessible heights. Work in an N-sort containing S_n and S_{n+1}. S_n is transitive, satisfies ZFC (it is isomorphic to M_n), and by Step 3 is closed under true power sets. By induction on α the internal rank hierarchy of S_n agrees with the true one (successor by power-set correctness, limits by union), and since S_n ⊨ "every set has a rank", S_n = V(δ_n) where δ_n is the ordinal height of S_n, a limit ordinal. This does not make δ_n inaccessible: a worldly cardinal has V(δ) ⊨ ZFC with true power sets and can be singular. Instead use P: axiom 2 of P in sort n+1 says κ_n is inaccessible and j_n maps P_n onto V(κ_n). Apply e_{n+1}. It sends j_n[D_n] to e_n[D_n] = S_n = V(δ_n) by Step 2, and it sends the internal V(κ_n) of P_{n+1} to the internal rank segment of S_{n+1} at e_{n+1}(κ_n), which is the true rank segment by power-set correctness of S_{n+1}. Hence e_{n+1}(κ_n) = δ_n and S_{n+1} ⊨ "δ_n is inaccessible"; by consequence (b) this is absolute to the ambient N-sort. The same transport of axioms 3 and 4 of P gives: δ_{n+1} is the least inaccessible above δ_n, and δ_0 has no greatest inaccessible below it.

Step 5: the bottom label. First, δ_0 ≤ κ_0 (the κ_0 of N). Indeed δ_0 is an ordinal of some N_b, so δ_0 < κ_b in N_{b+1}. If δ_0 > κ_0, then by consequence (c) δ_0 is one of κ_1, …, κ_{b-1}, each of which has κ_{m} as its greatest inaccessible predecessor, contradicting Step 4 for δ_0. Only the finitely many named inaccessibles below κ_b enter; nothing is assumed about inaccessible limit points beyond them.

Suppose now δ_0 < κ_0. Then δ_n < κ_0 for every n, by external induction: δ_{n+1} is the least inaccessible above δ_n and κ_0 is an inaccessible above δ_n, so δ_{n+1} ≤ κ_0, and equality would make δ_n the greatest inaccessible below κ_0, against axiom 4 for N. Hence every S_n = V(δ_n) is a subset of V(κ_0) = j[N_0], so each h_n is an M-definable injection of M_n into N_0.

Now use finite support of I. The domain of N_0 in M is a quotient of a definable subset of a finite product of M-sorts of index ≤ K, for some K. In M_{K+1} that product is a subset of V(κ_K), so by Choice in M_{K+1} the domain of N_0 has cardinality at most |V(κ_K)|. The graph of h_{K+1} : M_{K+1} → N_0 is M-definable with finite support, hence a set of a high M-sort, and it is injective; so |V(κ_{K+1})| ≤ |V(κ_K)| there. But V(κ_{K+1}) contains the power set of V(κ_K), and Cantor's theorem gives the opposite strict inequality. Contradiction. Therefore δ_0 = κ_0.

Step 6: the isomorphism. Both (δ_n) and (κ_n) are sequences starting at the same inaccessible with each term the least inaccessible above the previous, so δ_n = κ_n for every n by external induction, and S_n = V(κ_n) = j[N_n]. Then j^{-1} ∘ h_n : M_n → N_n is an M-definable membership isomorphism; the family commutes with the transition maps by Step 2 and sends each κ_n of M to κ_n of N because e_{n+1}(κ_n) = δ_n. This is the required M-definable isomorphism M → N. ∎

Remarks. (i) External induction over n is legitimate because the sorts are externally indexed; no statement quantifies over n inside any theory. (ii) The proof never assumes any model is well-founded from outside. Step 1 uses the collapse theorem of N_b, not a collapse in an ambient universe. (iii) Effectiveness: given the formulas of I, J, i, the formulas for the h_n are computed uniformly; Step 5 fixes K from the support of I. (iv) Quotients. Definition 1.1 allows an interpretation to translate equality as a definable equivalence relation, and the proof never assumes otherwise: in Step 1 the domain of P_n modulo J-equality is a set of N_b by Lemma 1.3, and the collapse e_n is taken there, so the quotient is handled by Separation, Replacement and Choice in a ZFC sort. This is exactly the point at which the argument for KM in [Enayat, Theorem 2.7] has a gap, found by Gruza and Łełyk and recorded in [Enayat-C]: extending the ordinal-indexed partial isomorphisms across a successor step requires choosing representatives modulo the interpreted equality over a set-indexed family of classes, an instance of class collection (CC_set) that KM does not prove, and since KM + CC does not eliminate imaginaries the quotient case cannot be assumed away; what the argument proves is that KM + CC_set is solid, and the solidity of KM itself is open. In H every interpreted sort is set-sized in a higher sort, so no class collection is needed. The machine-checked statement quantifies over interpretations whose equality is an arbitrary definable equivalence (`SortInterp.eqv`, §8), so the case that failed for KM is covered by the formal proof.

## 3. The calculus L_ann

### 3.1 Design principle

The set interpretation of a term must know whether the term is a proof (interpreted by the single proof object) or data, and at what universe its type lives. Carneiro's semantics recovers this from typing derivations and then needs a coherence theorem saying different derivations agree, which is where unique typing enters. Writing a classifier on every term, including variables, avoids derivations but does not survive conversion: a tag on a variable goes stale under context conversion, as a self-instantiation counterexample shows. Annotations are therefore stored only where they are decided, at binders and applications, universe levels are kept in the context, and the classifier is a function computed from those.

Universes. U_n for n ∈ ω; U_0 is Prop; U_n : U_{n+1}; no cumulativity. Explicit lifts move between levels.

Expressions.
- variables x; constants c (ground instances of declarations; universe-polymorphic declarations are effective families of ground instances);
- U_n;
- products Π[k; i, j](x : A). B with a kind k ∈ {prop, data}, i the level of A, j the level of B; k = prop forces j = 0; the product lives at level 0 if k = prop and at max(i, j) otherwise;
- abstractions λ[k; i, j](x : A). b with the same data;
- applications app[k; j](f, a);
- let[j](x : A := v). b;
- the primitive constants of §3.4, each with its declared ground type.

Variables and constants carry nothing. A context is a list x_1 : A_1 @ i_1, …, x_m : A_m @ i_m; each binder records the level of its type.

Classifier. For a context Γ and expression e, define c(Γ, e), the level of e's type, by recursion on e: c(Γ, x) is the level recorded for x in Γ; c(Γ, U_n) = n + 2; c(Γ, Π[k; i, j]…) is 1 if k = prop and max(i, j) + 1 otherwise; c(Γ, λ[k; i, j]…) is 0 if k = prop and max(i, j) otherwise; c(Γ, app[k; j](f, a)) is 0 if k = prop and j otherwise; c(Γ, let[j] …) = j; for a constant, the level of its declared type. Write ℓ(Γ, A) := c(Γ, A) − 1 for the level of a type expression.

Judgments. Γ ⊢ ok; Γ ⊢ t : A @ r; Γ ⊢ t ≡ s : A @ r. The convention is that r is the level of the type: Γ ⊢ t : A @ r presupposes Γ ⊢ A : U_r @ r+1, so that c(Γ, t) = r and ℓ(Γ, A) = r. So U_n : U_{n+1} @ n+2, a proposition P : U_0 @ 1, and a proof h : P @ 0.

### 3.2 Rules

Context formation extends Γ by x : A @ i when Γ ⊢ A : U_i @ i+1 and x is fresh. Typing rules: variable (level read from Γ), universe, constant (level of the closed declared type), product formation for both kinds, abstraction, application with result type B[a/x] at level j, let, weakening, substitution, and conversion. Conversion is typed and level-preserving: from Γ ⊢ t : A @ r and Γ ⊢ A ≡ B : U_r @ r+1 infer Γ ⊢ t : B @ r; context conversion replaces a binder x : A @ i by x : B @ i for Γ ⊢ A ≡ B : U_i @ i+1. Definitional equality is the typed congruence closure generated by β for both kinds, η for data functions, ζ, δ for definitions, proof irrelevance at level 0, and the certified ground equations of the environment (primitive computation rules and recursor equations). Congruence compares constructors with identical annotations. All substitution is capture-avoiding and certified as such; on named syntax this is a side condition on each β, ζ and substitution step, and nothing more.

Lemma 3.2 (classifier coherence). If Γ ⊢ t : A @ r then c(Γ, t) = r and ℓ(Γ, A) = r. If Γ ⊢ t ≡ s : A @ r then c(Γ, t) = c(Γ, s) = r.

Sketch. Simultaneous induction on derivations. Variable: c reads Γ, and context formation recorded the level of A from a derivation, so the induction hypothesis applies to it. Universe, product, abstraction, application, let: the rule writes exactly the annotation that c reads. Substitution t[a/x]: the binder x : A @ i and the argument a with Γ ⊢ a : A @ i have the same classifier by the induction hypothesis, and c is preserved by replacing occurrences of x by an expression of the same classifier; this is a syntactic lemma on c proved by induction on t. Conversion and context conversion: r and the binder level are unchanged by the rule. β: app[k; j](λ[k; i, j](x : A). b, a) has classifier j, and b[a/x] has classifier c((Γ, x : A @ i), b) = j by the abstraction premise. η, ζ, proof irrelevance, and ground equations: immediate from typed endpoints, provided each registered ground equation is checked for classifier agreement at registration, which is a decidable syntactic test. Congruence: identical annotations. ∎

The lemma is what makes the interpretation in §4 well defined on raw annotated syntax: the evaluator reads kinds off binders and applications, never needs a derivation, and Lemma 3.2 guarantees that a term typed at a proposition is erased and a term typed at data is not, whichever derivation is used. A stale-tag counterexample is not expressible here because a variable has no tag to go stale; the level lives in the binder that context conversion leaves unchanged.

### 3.3 What is still unproved about the calculus

(i) Lemma 3.2 itself. (ii) Level determinacy of definitional equality: Γ ⊢ U_r ≡ U_s : U_m @ m+1 implies r = s. It cannot be proved semantically, because the intended model is cumulative and V(κ_r) ⊆ V(κ_s) for r < s; it needs a syntactic argument or a model with pairwise distinct universe objects, for instance the raw-set model with universes tagged by level. It is needed only to show the annotation of U_n cannot be changed by conversion, which Lemma 3.2 already forbids on the term side; whether it is needed at all should be settled before formalization. (iii) Decidability of the certified judgments is not claimed and not needed: certificates are finite derivation trees and are checked, not searched for.

### 3.4 Primitives, inductives, logic

The stock: False; dependent sums with projections and pair reconstruction; finite sums with dependent elimination; explicit lifts with reconstruction; propositional truncation eliminating into Prop; W-types with dependent recursion; equality with dependent elimination; accessibility with well-founded recursion into data; quotients by a relation with constructor, soundness and respectful lift, Type-valued quotients taken modulo the least equivalence closure; and the indexed inductive discipline (recursive fields are products ending in the family; other argument types and indices do not depend on recursive values; Prop-valued families eliminate into Type only with at most one constructor whose nonproof arguments are recoverable from the indices), with the corresponding K-like equations. Logical axioms: propositional extensionality, excluded middle, propositional Choice (existence of a choice function, not a chosen one), and unique choice as a data primitive. There is no unconstrained Classical.choice. Every primitive constant carries its ground type with annotations, and every computation rule is registered with a classifier check per Lemma 3.2. Nested inductives, structure eta beyond single-constructor records, and universe-level quantification are outside the calculus.

## 4. The interpretation F of L_ann in H

The clauses are those of Carneiro's model; the form of the claim is what matters here: soundness is stated as a theorem scheme of H, with the definability requirement explicit.

### 4.1 What is being claimed

F is not a construction performed on one intended model. It is a family of H-formulas: for every context Γ and annotated expression t there is a formula G_{Γ,t}(z̄, w), in finitely many sorts of H determined by the universe levels occurring in Γ and t, such that H proves that G_{Γ,t} is the graph of a function on the valid environments of Γ. "F(A)" for a model A of H is then the structure these formulas define in A, and every property of F(A) asserted below is an H-theorem about the formulas, one per certified judgment. An abstract semantics built on metatheoretic function spaces does not provide this discipline; it is also why every clause is asserted for valid environments only, since a set graph satisfies β on its domain and nowhere else.

### 4.2 Sorts and environments

The type sort of level n+1 of F is the whole sort U_n of H: every set of U_n is the code of a data type at level n+1 (this is the raw-set policy; see §6.4). Prop is the two-element set {∅, {∅}} of truth values, formed in U_0; the proof object is ∗ := ∅. A context Γ = x_1 : A_1 @ i_1, …, x_m : A_m @ i_m denotes the set of valid environments: tuples η with η(x_k) ∈ G_{A_k}(η restricted to earlier variables); a variable at level 0 takes the value ∗. This set lives in a sort above every i_k. Every clause below is asserted for valid environments only; a graph's values off its domain are never used and never specified.

### 4.3 Clauses

For a valid environment η:
- Variables: η(x). Constants: their fixed set (§3.4, and the inductive construction for compiled families).
- U_n: the set j[U_n] as an element of U_{n+1}; U_0: the set of truth values.
- Π[prop](x : A). P: the truth value of "for every a in the value of A, the value of P at (η, a) is {∅}". Π[data](x : A). B: the internal dependent product, formed in the sort of level max(i, j)+1 as the set of all functions f with domain the value of A such that f(a) belongs to the value of B at (η, a). That this set exists and belongs to the right sort is the closure of inaccessible rank segments under dependent products.
- λ[prop]: ∗. λ[data](x : A). b: the graph {(a, value of b at (η, a)) : a in the value of A}, a set by Replacement in the sort.
- app[prop]: ∗. app[data](f, a): the unique w with (value of a, w) in the value of f.
- let: the value of the body at the extended environment.
- Primitives: dependent sums as sets of pairs; finite sums as tagged unions with fixed tags; lifts as the identity on carriers; truncation as the truth value of nonemptiness; equality as the truth value of set equality, with elimination returning the supplied value; W-types as sets of well-founded constructor trees with recursion by well-founded induction; accessibility as the truth value of accessibility, with data recursion by well-founded recursion on the accessible part; Type-valued quotients as sets of equivalence classes under the least equivalence closure, with lift by the uniquely determined common value; Prop-valued quotients as the truth value of the original proposition.
- Indexed inductives: fibers, over the indices, of the set of good trees (well-founded trees of constructor applications obeying the discipline of §3.4), Prop-valued families as truth values of existence of a good tree, large elimination for the admitted subsingleton case by uniqueness of the good tree.

### 4.4 Substitution and coherence

For a certified substitution σ and any t, H proves that the value of t[σ] at η equals the value of t at σ(η); structural induction on annotated syntax, with capture-avoiding substitution and environment extension agreeing under binders. Because the evaluator is defined on annotated raw terms, there is no coherence question between derivations of the same judgment; Lemma 3.2 is what guarantees that the annotations a derivation certifies are the ones the evaluator reads.

Theorem 4.1 (soundness, as an H-scheme). There is a primitive recursive function from derivation certificates to H-proofs such that:
- for a certificate of Γ ⊢ ok, H proves that the environment set of Γ is the dependent tuple set described in 4.2;
- for a certificate of Γ ⊢ t : A @ r, H proves that for every valid η the value of t belongs to the value of A, and that it equals ∗ when r = 0;
- for a certificate of Γ ⊢ t ≡ s : A @ r, H proves that the two values agree on every valid η;
- for a context conversion, H proves the two environment sets are equal.

Sketch. Induction on the certificate: variables and structural rules from 4.4; universes by axiom 2 of H; products by closure of rank segments; abstraction and application from the graph clauses on the domain; conversion is immediate because the premise supplies equality of the two type values and the term's value does not change; β by 4.4; η because a graph with the declared domain is recovered by evaluation; proof irrelevance and proof-η because ∗ is the only proof object; the primitive and inductive equations from the fixed constructions; the logical axioms from classical logic on truth values, Choice in the sort, and unique-value selection. Each case yields an H-proof uniformly in the certificate, so the map from certificates to proofs is primitive recursive. ∎

### 4.5 Consequence for the relational presentation

Read the symbols of the theory T_L of §6.3 through F: each graph symbol R_{Γ,t} is interpreted by G_{Γ,t}. Theorem 4.1 says exactly that H proves the totality, single-valuedness and equation axioms of T_L that concern the old calculus. This is the statement "F is a finitary interpretation of the old part of T_L in H", and it is a scheme, not a single sentence, because T_L has a symbol per certified judgment. The support of G_{Γ,t} is bounded by the largest level in Γ, t, plus one; no formula ranges over all sorts.

## 5. The internal set tower, L_0, the extraction D, and the round trip

### 5.1 Internal sets

Inside L_ann, for each n define PSet_n := W(A : U_{n+1}). A, the W-type of trees whose nodes are indexed by types of level n+1; extensional equivalence by recursion on trees; Z_n := PSet_n modulo that equivalence, a Type-valued quotient at level n+2; membership on Z_n by quotient elimination and propositional extensionality; the lift L_n : Z_n → Z_{n+1} by tree recursion and quotient elimination (it re-indexes a tree by the lifted index type). These are ordinary L_ann definitions, one per n.

### 5.2 L_0

Add constants b_n : Z_{n+1} and, for each axiom of H, its translation to a proposition of L_ann about the structure (Z_n, ∈, L_n, b_n): universal quantifiers over sort U_n become quantifiers over Z_n, j_n becomes L_n, κ_n becomes b_n. Call the result L_0. Axiom 1 (Z_n satisfies ZFC) is largely provable in L_ann already: extensionality, pairing, union, power set, infinity, foundation and separation follow the Mathlib development of ZFSet; replacement and choice need a remark. Given f : Z_n → Z_n and x, propositional Choice yields the existence of a function selecting a representative tree for each f-value on the children of a representative of x, hence the existence of a tree whose class has the required members; extensionality makes that class unique, and unique choice then names it. So Replacement and the set form of Choice are provable without Classical.choice. Nothing below depends on which instances are provable, since L_0 postulates all of them.

### 5.3 The extraction D

D interprets H in L_0: sort U_n ↦ the elements of the type Z_n (one sort of the relational presentation of §6.3, namely the fiber over the type object Z_n), ∈ ↦ internal membership, j_n ↦ L_n, κ_n ↦ b_n. It is finitary with support one sort per H-sort and no parameters. L_0 proves D(φ) for every axiom φ of H by construction of L_0. This is the only place where the tower axioms enter the Lean-side theory, and it is by postulation, not by proof.

### 5.4 The round trip

Fix a model A of H and read F(A). Its internal Z_n is the set of equivalence classes of well-founded trees whose index sets are elements of U_n, a set of U_{n+2}. Define in U_{n+1} the collapse c_n : trees → sets by recursion, c_n(tree) = { c_n(child) : child }, and observe that a tree with index sets in V(κ_n) collapses to an element of V(κ_n) because κ_n is inaccessible (the collapse is hereditarily of size below κ_n). Equivalent trees have equal collapses and conversely, so c_n descends to an injection ε_n : Z_n^{F(A)} → j[U_n]. It is onto: for x ∈ V(κ_n), the canonical tree T_x indexed by x itself with children T_y for y ∈ x, defined by ∈-recursion in U_{n+1}, collapses to x. ε_n preserves and reflects membership by the definition of internal membership, commutes with the lifts because L_n re-indexes without changing the collapse, and sends the interpretation of b_n to κ_n once F interprets b_n as the class of the canonical tree of κ_n. All of this is provable in the sort U_{n+2} of H for each n separately, and the maps have support two sorts. Hence:

Proposition 5.1. H proves, for each n, that ε_n is an isomorphism between sort n of D(F(A)) and sort n of A, compatible with transitions and constants. So ε : D(F(A)) → A is an H-definable isomorphism in the sense of §1, and F, extended by b_n ↦ class of T_{κ_n}, is an interpretation of L_0 in H (the translated H axioms hold in F(A) because ε transports them from A).

### 5.5 On universe separation

A single formal universe cannot host an increasing sequence of exact inaccessible cutoffs. In H that is not a difficulty but a feature: sort U_n is not a definable cutoff inside itself, and the constructions of 5.4 for level n are carried out in U_{n+1} and U_{n+2}. What a formalization must respect is that each source level is its own sort; the paper needs no further device.

## 6. The representation scheme, L_solid, and its relational presentation

The relational presentation T_L (§6.3) is the theory whose solidity is claimed; its adequacy claim (Claim 6.1) is stated and sketched. The policy discussion (§6.4) is expository.

### 6.1 The scheme

For each n add constants code_n : U_{n+1} → Z_n and value_n : (A : U_{n+1}) → A → Z_n, both at level n+2. (Here U_{n+1} is Type n.) Write, for a context Γ and a valid assignment γ, the encoding ρ(γ): a variable x : A @ i with i ≥ 1 is sent to value_{i−1}(A(γ), γ(x)) and a variable at level 0 to ∗; a term t with Γ ⊢ t : A @ r is encoded by value_{r−1}(A(γ), t(γ)) for r ≥ 1 and by ∗ for r = 0. The axioms, as native propositions of L_0 at finite levels:
- code_n is injective and every z : Z_n is code_n(A) for some A. (Existential surjectivity; no inverse is named.)
- For every A : U_{n+1}, value_n(A) is injective and its image is exactly the set of members of code_n(A).
- value_{n+1}(U_{n+1}, A) = L_n(code_n(A)) for every A : U_{n+1}.
- code_0(Prop) is the set of the two truth values, and for every P : Prop, P holds iff value_0(Prop, P) is the true value.
- For every derivation certificate of a judgment Γ ⊢ t : A @ r of L_0, with G_{Γ,t} the H-formula of §4 read in L_0 through D (its quantifiers range over the Z_m):

- Normalization: for every z : Z_n, C_n(value_{n+1}(Z_n, z), L_n(z)), where C_n is the graph, definable in L_0, of the collapse of the internal raw-set model built over Z_{n+1} (the internal analogue of §5.4).

In 5, γ ranges over all valid assignments to Γ, including elements of the sorts that no closed term denotes; this is what makes the scheme bite in arbitrary models. L_solid is L_0 with the new constants and axioms 1–6. The axiom set is recursive: 1–4 and 6 are one sentence per n, and 5 is indexed by certificates, which are finite trees checked syntactically.

### 6.2 What the scheme says

Axioms 1 and 2 make (A, a) ↦ (code_n(A), value_n(A, a)) a bijection from the total space of typed elements at level n+1 onto pairs (s, z) with z ∈ s in Z_n. Axiom 3 identifies the two ways a type appears, as a code and as an element of the next universe. Axiom 5 says the encoding carries every certified operation to its set-theoretic description. Axiom 6 pins down how the encoding acts on the internal set tower itself, which is what lets §7 recover the new constants.

### 6.3 The relational presentation T_L

T_L is a many-sorted first-order theory. Sorts: B_n for n ≥ 0 (type objects at level n+1), E_n (typed elements at level n+1), Ω (propositions), with projections π_n : E_n → B_n and a truth predicate Tr on Ω; proofs are not a sort, since proof irrelevance makes a true proposition's proof unique. Symbols: the universe objects u_n ∈ B_{n+1} and the identification of B_n with the fiber of E_{n+1} over u_n; the application relation App_n on E-sorts; one relation symbol per primitive former and constant of §3.4, on its declared domain; and the relations Code_n ⊆ B_n × E_{n+1} and Value_n ⊆ E_n × E_{n+1} whose second coordinates lie in the fiber over the type object of Z_n. Every certified expression t in context Γ has a T_L-definable graph ⟦t⟧_Γ, by structural recursion from these symbols; the context domain D_Γ is likewise definable by recursion on Γ. The axioms of T_L are: (a) fiber and projection axioms, propositional extensionality and excluded middle on Ω; (b) totality and single-valuedness of every primitive relation on its declared domain; (c) comprehension, one instance per certified abstraction Γ, x : A @ i ⊢ b : B @ j with j ≥ 1: for every γ ∈ D_Γ there is a unique f in the product object with App(f, a) = ⟦b⟧(γ, a) for all a in the domain; (d) one equation per certified Γ ⊢ t ≡ s : A @ r, namely ∀γ ∈ D_Γ . ⟦t⟧(γ) = ⟦s⟧(γ); (e) the translations of the H axioms for the internal tower (the L_0 axioms) and the schemes 1–6, with code_n and value_n rendered by Code_n and Value_n. All axioms are finite sentences with finite sort support, and the axiom set is recursive.

Claim 6.1 (adequacy). (i) For every certificate of Γ ⊢ t : A @ r in L_solid, T_L proves that ⟦t⟧_Γ is total and single-valued on D_Γ with values in the fiber over ⟦A⟧_Γ (or in Ω, or equal to the unique proof, according to r). (ii) If L_solid derives a closed proposition φ, then T_L proves Tr(⟦φ⟧). (iii) Conversely, every model of T_L validates every derivable judgment of L_solid under ⟦·⟧.

Sketch. (i) is induction on certificates; the rule cases are the axioms (b)–(d) and Lemma 3.2 is used to know which clause applies. (ii) follows from (i) applied to a proof term of φ, whose value lies in the proof fiber, which is nonempty exactly when Tr holds. (iii) is (i) read semantically. ∎

The claim is what connects "L_solid is solid" to the calculus a user writes in. It is listed as an obligation in §8.

### 6.4 The type-identity policy

Under axioms 1 and 2 every set of Z_n is the code of a type and every type is coded by a set, and two types are equal exactly when their codes are, since code_n is injective. For closed types, axiom 5 fixes the codes: the code of an inductive type is the set its compiled W-type construction produces, the code of a function type is the set of graphs, and so on. Consequently L_solid decides the equalities that ordinary Lean leaves open, and decides them by the encoding: Nat and Int have different codes, and so do Bool → Unit and Nat → Unit, whose codes are the singletons of two different graphs. Transport is determined as well: for h : A = B, axiom 5 for the equality eliminator says value_n(B, cast h a) = value_n(A, a), so casts are identities on encoded values. This is the raw-set policy of Carneiro's model, and it is close in practice to "distinct unless forced": identifications occur only where two constructions produce the same set.

The policy is a choice, not a consequence of the architecture. §7 uses only that F is an interpretation with a definable round trip; a structural policy would replace axiom 1 by a bijection onto a definable set of codes C_n ⊆ Z_n with a decoding relation, and would need F built from those codes. What Cantor forbids is the naive form of such a policy, injectivity of every type constructor in all its parameters, as the example of a type with a parameter of type U_1 → Prop shows; identification rules are then unavoidable, which is the design work that taking raw sets avoids. The decision belongs to the specification, and the paper records it rather than argues for it.

## 7. Bi-interpretability, solidity, and proposition reduction

Every step in this section is a derivation in H or in L_solid. The arguments are short once §§4–6 are in place; their weight rests on those sections.

### 7.1 F interprets L_solid in H

Extend F to the new constants by the canonical expansion: code_n := ε_n^{-1} on type objects and value_n(B, b) := ε_n^{-1}(b) on elements, where ε^{-1} is the definable inverse of §5.4 (the class of the canonical tree). H proves each axiom of L_solid in this expansion, for each instance separately: axiom 1 because ε_n is a bijection; axiom 2 because ε_n preserves and reflects membership; axiom 3 by compatibility of ε with the lifts; axiom 4 because ε sends the two truth values to the two truth values; axiom 5 because G_{Γ,t} read through D in F(A) is G_{Γ,t} evaluated in the structure D(F(A)), which ε identifies with A, and in A the formula holds of any environment and the value of t by the definition of G (satisfaction is invariant under the definable isomorphism ε, an H-theorem per formula); axiom 6 by naturality of ε with respect to definable isomorphisms of towers, applied to ε itself, which gives ε_{D(F(A))} ∘ D(F(ε^{-1})) = ε^{-1} ∘ ε = id. Hence:

Proposition 7.1. F is a finitary interpretation of T_L in H. In particular T_L, and so L_solid, is consistent relative to H, and every model of H carries a canonical model of L_solid.

### 7.2 ρ is an isomorphism of old structures, provably in L_solid

Read F(D(M)) inside a model M of T_L: its type sort at level n+1 is Z_n, its element sort is the set of pairs (s, z) with z ∈ s, its application is graph evaluation, its primitives are their set constructions, its propositions are the truth values. Define ρ on B_n by code_n, on E_n by value_n, and on Ω by value_0(Prop, ·). Then T_L proves, sort by sort: ρ is a bijection on B_n and on E_n (axioms 1, 2) and on Ω (axiom 4); ρ respects the universe identification (axiom 3); ρ preserves App and every primitive relation (axiom 5 applied to the certificate of the corresponding typing judgment, whose G-formula is precisely the set-theoretic clause of that operation); ρ maps each context domain D_Γ bijectively onto the valid environments of F(D(M)), by induction on the length of Γ using bijectivity of value_n on each fiber; and ρ preserves Tr (axiom 4). A bijection that preserves functional relations reflects them. So ρ is an isomorphism between the reduct of M to the old symbols and the reduct of F(D(M)).

### 7.3 ρ preserves the new constants

F(D(M)) carries its canonical expansion, code^can := ε_{D(M)}^{-1} and value^can := ε_{D(M)}^{-1}, where ε_{D(M)} is the round trip of §5.4 built over the internal tower of M; Proposition 5.1 transports along D, so L_0 proves ε_{D(M)} is an isomorphism. ρ preserves Code_n if value_{n+1}(Z_n, code_n(A)) = ε_{D(M)}^{-1}(L_n(code_n(A))) for every A, and preserves Value_n if value_{n+1}(Z_n, value_n(A, a)) = ε_{D(M)}^{-1}(L_n(value_n(A, a))); both are instances of axiom 6 with z := code_n(A) and z := value_n(A, a). (Axiom 6 is stated with the collapse graph C_n rather than ε^{-1} so that no inverse is chosen; the two forms are equivalent since the collapse is a bijection.) The presentation issue of function-valued constants versus graphs does not arise in T_L, where Code_n and Value_n are relations.

Theorem 7.2. T_L and H are bi-interpretable via (F, D, ε, ρ). Hence, by Theorem 2.1 and Lemma 1.2, T_L is solid in the category of §1.

### 7.4 Proposition reduction, provably

Let Γ ⊢ φ : U_0 @ 1 be a certified proposition in context, and let

a formula over the internal sorts Z_m with the internal membership. Then L_solid proves ∀γ ∈ Γ . φ(γ) ↔ φ^set(ρ(γ)). Proof. Axiom 5 for the certificate of Γ ⊢ φ : U_0 gives G_{Γ,φ}(ρ(γ), value_0(Prop, φ(γ))); G is single-valued (an H-theorem transported by D into L_0); and axiom 4 says φ(γ) holds iff value_0(Prop, φ(γ)) is the true value. ∎ No induction on the structure of φ is needed, because axiom 5 already covers compound propositions; a connective-by-connective argument would be correct but redundant. The translation is effective in the certificate, uses finitely many levels, and can be expressed in a single Z_m by Lemma 1.3 read through D.

### 7.5 Conservativity in the interpretation sense

For a sentence ψ of H: H ⊢ ψ iff L_solid ⊢ D(ψ). If H ⊢ ψ then L_0 ⊢ D(ψ), because L_0 proves D of every H axiom and D preserves derivations. If L_solid ⊢ D(ψ) then H ⊢ F(D(ψ)), and H ⊢ F(D(ψ)) ↔ ψ because ε is an H-definable isomorphism D(F(A)) → A. So the set-theoretic content of L_solid is exactly H.

### 7.6 What the theorem says to a user of the calculus

Under Claim 6.1, every proposition provable in L_solid is true in every model of T_L; every proposition has a provably equivalent set-theoretic form; the type equalities and transports that ordinary Lean leaves open are decided; and the models of T_L are, up to definable isomorphism, the models of H. What it does not say: anything about Lean 4's kernel, elaborator, nested inductives or structure eta, which are outside L_ann; and anything about a computational reading of L_solid, whose new axioms have no reduction behavior.

## 8. Status

Nothing here has been refereed. "Checked" means: a theorem of the Lean 4 development `Solid/` in the repository github.com/elliotglazer/solid-lean (Lean 4.34.0, Mathlib v4.34.0, the Foundation library for first-order logic, no `sorry`, axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound` only), stated for arbitrary models of H with a class system, never for standard models. Theorem names are given in monospace; file names are relative to `Solid/`.

**The formalized presentation.** The development takes T_L in the *set-encoded* signature: the sorts of T_L are the tower sorts Z_n themselves, and T_L is H together with one relation symbol R_{Γ,t} per context Γ and term t, each with the defining axiom that R_{Γ,t} is the evaluator clause G_{Γ,t} of §4.3 (`Calc/Theory.lean`: `LAnn`). In this presentation D is the reduct to the tower signature, F is the expansion by the defining clauses, and ε and ρ are identities; so §§5–7 collapse to two general theorems about expansions of H by clause-defined symbols: every model of H expands uniquely to a model of the expansion (`Gen/Clauses.lean`: `expand_isGenModel`), and the expansion is solid (`Gen/Expansion.lean`: `ClauseFamily.solid`). The presentation of §6.3, with sorts B_n, E_n, Ω, is recovered from the encoded one by a second general theorem: an expansion of a solid theory by definable sorts — new sorts that are copies of definable subsets of old sorts, with an encoding symbol each, and further symbols defined by clauses on the codes — is again solid and has the same models (`Gen/SortSolid.lean`: `SortExp.solid`; `Gen/SortExpand.lean`: `SortExp.expand_isModel`). The instance is `Calc/BE.lean`: B_r is the set of type objects at level r (Ω = B_0), E_r the set of typed elements ⟨A, j a⟩, and Code_r, Value_r are the encoding symbols of these sorts. What is *not* formalized is the internal construction of the set tower inside L_ann (§5.1–5.3) and the round trip ε (§5.4): the formal theory has the tower as primitive sorts. Consequently the formal theorem is about the relational theory T_L, not about L_solid as an extension of L_ann by the constants code_n, value_n; the passage between the two is the content of §§5–6 and remains at sketch level. Derivability is formal as well: `Solid/FO/` writes the axioms of H and of T_L as many-sorted sentences, renders them in the single-sorted first-order logic of the Foundation library, and proves that derivability in its sequent calculus from the rendered axioms is exactly truth in every semantic model (`FO/Render.lean`: `provable_iff_semantic`); the provability claims of §§4, 6.3 and 7.5 below are obtained from the semantic theorems in this way.

**Where the formalization refines the text.** (1) The Nat-rec successor rule: the step function is applied at sort max(j, j), not j. (2) Side conditions: Σ, sums, quotients, Nat-rec and choice require level ≥ 1 where their result is data (`Prim.Ok`). (3) The product clauses of §4.3 carry the guard famOkF (the family is a function on the domain into the universe), which is what lets the application case reason about the codomain from the value of the type of f. (4) Ω = B_0: propositions are the type objects at level 0, so the B_n/E_n presentation needs no separate sort Ω. (5) Code_n and Value_n are the encoding symbols of the new sorts, and axiom 6 is void once the tower is primitive. (6) Step 5b uses no choice function and no Cantor; Step 2 is by foundation, not by induction over P.

**Obligations remaining.** (1) The primitive stock beyond the formalized one: W-types, accessibility and the indexed inductive discipline, each needing a clause, its set construction in a model of H, and a soundness case. (2) Derivations as objects: Theorem 4.1 and Claim 6.1 (i)–(ii) are proved as provability statements by completeness (`FO/Provable.lean`), so the derivations exist but are not computed; the primitive recursive proof-producing function of Theorem 4.1 is not formalized, and no certified derivation inside L_ann is constructed (in particular dne is kept as a primitive rather than derived by Diaconescu's argument from choice, propext and the quotient rules). (3) The identification of the paper's T_L (symbols for primitives, graphs by recursion) with the formal one (a symbol per term). (4) §5 in its own right — the internal tower and the round trip — if one wants L_solid as an extension of L_ann by constants rather than the relational theory T_L. (5) The finite-tuple normalization of §1.2, if solidity is to be stated for interpretations with tuple domains. Items 1 and 4 are the mathematical ones; 2, 3 and 5 are standard.
